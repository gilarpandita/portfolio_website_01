TokoBli Campaign Evaluation & Product Page Analysis

TokoBli is a fictional Indonesian e-commerce company (the project is for educational purposes) that runs its biggest sales events on “twin dates”: 10.10, 11.11 and 12.12. Each campaign has its own discount scenario plus free shipping, and transaction volume can jump to as much as ten times normal levels. Because every campaign costs real discount budget, management needs to know which scenario actually performs best, and whether the money spent on discounts is being used efficiently. This project was built in two parts. The first part evaluates the three past campaigns using a transaction dataset of 10,159 rows and 14 columns. I framed the business problem with the SMART criteria, selected the metrics that matter for comparing campaigns (total revenue, average order value, revenue per customer, transaction count, discount cost and units sold), cleaned the data in Google Sheets, removed outliers using the IQR method on Total Revenue, and calculated a full set of descriptive statistics for Quantity, Discount and Total Revenue. The cleaned dataset of 10,142 rows then fed an exploratory analysis that compared the campaigns and product categories on scale, cost and discount efficiency. The second part is an A/B test of a redesigned product detail page. One thousand users were split into two groups (498 saw the current page, 502 saw the new design), and I tested whether the new page changes transaction value in a statistically significant way. That meant checking the test assumptions, running a two-sample t-test, confirming the result with a non-parametric test, and measuring effect size, not just significance. The final output is a 26-slide deck with business recommendations structured in the OBIPR framework (Observation, Business Impact, Isolation, Prioritization, Recommendation).

What the Data Showed

Data quality
the raw data needed real cleaning. One column (Status) was 99.48% empty and was dropped, 5 duplicate rows were removed, 3 missing values were rebuilt from the revenue formula, and 12 outliers above the IQR upper bound (Rp 14.1M) were removed.
Campaign 11.11 wins on value
it had the highest revenue (Rp 16.62B), the highest average order value (Rp 4.89M), the highest revenue per customer (Rp 10.61M) and the most unique customers (1,567).
Revenue gap is not about traffic
transaction counts were almost flat across campaigns (3,329 to 3,414), yet revenue differed by about 12%. The difference comes from how much each customer spends, not how many show up.
Campaign 12.12 wins on efficiency
it returned Rp 58.54 in revenue for every Rp 1 of discount, versus 54.92 for 11.11 and 53.83 for 10.10, while spending the least on discounts (Rp 252.8M).
Men’s Fashion is the standout category
it led revenue and unique customers in all three campaigns, and had the best discount efficiency in two of the three, finishing a close second in 10.10.
Discounts are under-used:
only 25% of orders carried any discount, and discounted orders averaged lower revenue (Rp 3.80M) than full-price orders (Rp 4.82M).
Baskets are small
82% of orders were single-unit, with an extremely right-skewed quantity distribution (skewness 17.01).
The new product page works
Group B averaged Rp 830,460 versus Rp 746,103 for Group A, a lift of +11.31% (t = 8.00, df = 998, p < 0.001, Cohen’s d = 0.51). The 95% confidence interval (Rp 63,670 to Rp 105,045) stayed entirely positive, and a Mann-Whitney U test reached the same conclusion.

Recommendations

01/
Blend two playbooks
combine 11.11’s customer reach and order value with 12.12’s disciplined discount spending, instead of treating them as separate strategies.
02/
Give Men’s Fashion priority
put it first in placement and promotional budget, since it is the most consistent performer on both revenue and discount efficiency.
03/
Rework discounts on low-return categories
test lower discount rates or different promo mechanics for categories with consistently weak revenue-to-discount ratios, such as Appliances, Kids & Baby and Superstore.
04/
Grow basket size, not just traffic
pilot threshold-based discounts (unlocked above a minimum basket value) and “buy more, save more” bundles, starting with repeat-purchase categories.
05/
Ship the new product page in stages
roll out from 50% to 100% of users and re-verify the lift during the next twin-date campaign, because the test ran in a normal two-week window, not a campaign peak.

What I delivered

Problem framing
Defined the business problem as a SMART question and selected the metrics that answer it.
Data cleaning in Google Sheets
Handled missing values, duplicates and data type corrections, with every removal and imputation documented.
Outlier detection
Applied the IQR method to Total Revenue across the full dataset and documented the bounds and removed rows.
Descriptive statistics
Calculated count, min, max, range, mean, median, mode, Q1, Q3, variance, standard deviation and skewness for Quantity, Discount and Total Revenue, each with a business interpretation.
EDA
Compared the three campaigns and 15 product categories on transactions, customers, units sold, revenue, discount budget and revenue-to-discount ratio.
A/B hypothesis test
Stated the hypotheses, checked normality and variance assumptions, ran the two-sample t-test, cross-checked with Mann-Whitney U, and reported confidence interval and effect size.
OBIPR recommendations
Turned the statistical findings into prioritized, actionable business recommendations.
Presentation deck
Built a 26-slide deck covering business overview, methodology, cleaning, analysis, hypothesis testing and a final recap.
