Reversing the Decline: Diagnosing a 53.5% Revenue Drop in Iowa's Wholesale Spirits Market

I analyzed four years of wholesale transactions from the Iowa Liquor Sales dataset (BigQuery public data) for Baugment, my fictional name for Rémy Cointreau USA. Revenue fell from $6.06M in 2021 to $2.82M in 2025. My goal was to find the cause and define actions to reach 5% growth in 2026. The problem. Revenue per active store dropped 58.4% while the store count grew 11.7%. I split the possible causes into three: category mix, order depth, and geography. Each got one KPI and one if-then hypothesis.The data fix that changed the answer. The vendor appeared in six variants of ID format and name spelling. Standard filters missed 43,613 rows, about 20% of transactions. After I fixed the filter in SQL, the revenue peak moved from 2022 to 2021 and the measured decline grew from 39.8% to 53.5%. I also handled blank county numbers, returns, and one extreme outlier, and I documented each decision.

What the data showed

Existing stores order less.
Transactions per store fell 46.2%, and bottles per transaction fell 33.8%.
The decline sits in one category.
Imported Brandies dropped 67.4%, while Cordials & Liqueurs grew 61.3%.
Geography splits the picture.
Linn (-61%) and Scott (-63.4%) fell hardest, and Polk held at -33.5%.
78.5% Brandies account all-time revenue
Brandies account for 78.5% of all-time revenue, which creates a portfolio risk.

Recommendations

01/
Re-engage Brandies accounts in the field (within 3 months)
Visit the Brandies accounts in Linn and Scott counties to bring them back.
02/
Review Brandies pricing against competitors (paired with Cordials lessons)
Compare Brandies pricing with competitors and apply what drove growth in Cordials.
03/
Diversify into Dry Gins and Gold Rum (6 to 12 months)
Build a presence in two growing categories to reduce dependence on Brandies.
